An AI judge that rules only on what no rule covers, fed by everything the engine works out

Status (2026-11-12): J0–J3 are built; J3 was live at cab821d on November 11. Its approval run exposed an exchange-consent defect (Q96). The exact offer lifecycle is specified but unbuilt and must be repaired before J5. Q76 now specifies J4's credential and norm representation; implementation remains unbuilt. Built machinery does not establish semantic adequacy; the extra model reviewer is deferred from the minimum required design.

1. The examples: follow one attempt

Blue, solid: built mechanism Orange, dashed: required behavior not yet demonstrated Grey: historical or explanatory context

The port. "A small port: Rosa, the customs officer, signs inspection releases. Vik, a trader, has 40 coins and a crate he has not declared. The exit gate is a barcode scanner that opens for any valid release code. Beyond the gate, a 100-step chain runs: the crate reaches the warehouse, the warehouse fills, the dispatcher sends a truck..." Pick a case; the steps it passes through light up on the left.

1. A person tries somethinghave
A Concordia resident says what it tries in its own words; the interpreter reads it against the installed moves.
↓
2. Does a rule cover it?have
If an installed move fits, the rulebook decides, as today; if one of its checks fails, the attempt is refused with the blocker named and the judge is not asked. An uncovered attempt can now reach the judge.
↓
3. Pass 1: what the engine knows without a rulebuilt, J1
Nothing has happened yet, so there is nothing to propagate. The engine lists the moves open to the person acting and to each person the attempt names, with which checks pass or fail (engine.discover for each), and the current values of every thing the attempt names. In the port, Rosa's list holds "sign release: blocked, inspected is false"; Vik's list could not show it.
↓
4. Someone else's choice goes to themJ2 routing built; Q96 open
If the attempt needs another person to act (accept, sign, refuse), that person's own resident decides. J2 routes choices; the complete per-offer consent guard remains unbuilt (Q96).
↓
5. The judge proposes a rulebuilt, J2
Concordia's own game master, given the whole world, the attempt and pass 1, proposes a ruling on what the world makes possible (can coins change hands here?), written as a rule that applies to everyone in that situation. It never writes an outcome.
↓
6. Pass 2: the engine test-runs that rulebuilt, J0 and J1
The rules check, then a test run on a copy of the world: every knock-on effect, however long the chain, and every blocker. The judge reads the result and confirms, revises once, or withdraws. The semantic benefit of this extra model step remains unvalidated. If no rule the language allows can express the ruling, the attempt is refused: "no rule could express this".
↓
7. The rule is installed, or heldbuilt, J3
J3 installs a checked rule by default or holds it for approval. Approval never supplies another person’s consent. Q96 requires live exact terms, matching acceptance and fresh feasibility before exchange settlement; this lifecycle is unbuilt.
↓
8. From then on the rulebook decides ithave, with the new rule; J0 lifts the chain cap
The rule can cover later attempts without a judge call; each exchange still needs its own consent (Q96, unbuilt); every knock-on runs to the end in the same moment, each step citing its cause.

2. What exists and what remains

J0 propagates uncapped causal chains and preserves historical replay. J1–J2 provide consequence reports and Concordia judge proposals. J3 installs or holds checked rulings. These mechanisms have run evidence; they do not establish semantic fidelity. Complete exchange consent (Q96), credentials and breakable norms (J4), and natural-language stopping (J5) remain unbuilt.

5. Architecture

The engine owns atomic effects and propagation (J0). Consequence reports belong to src/world_substrate/consequences.py (J1); Concordia’s EventResolution is integrated through scripts/judge.py (J2). scripts/rulings.py owns reusable rules (J3). The planned Q96 lifecycle belongs to the run coordinator and sealed continuation state: it must bind immutable parties, terms, expiry and rule fingerprint on every settlement path, including installed-rule reuse and delayed approval. Discovery filtering alone cannot enforce consent.

6. Semantic spectrum ladder

The semantic spectrum links domain, type, system and executable models. The package checks below report coverage; a populated rung does not prove runtime fidelity.

Credential contract (Q76, designed; unbuilt). A symbolic credential value, a person’s access to it, physical token custody, the current verifier and normative permission are different records. Disclosure can copy information; revocation can invalidate remembered information. Delivery does not imply trust or willingness to act. The design contract specifies visibility and enforcement boundaries; A6 lists the counterexamples an implementation must demonstrate.

7. Slices and remaining work

J0–J3 are built. The J3 approval run exposed Q96: a reusable coin rule allowed taking another person’s coins without an offer. Repair its offer lifecycle before J5. Matching acceptance may wait for an exact rule but never extends expiry. Approval cannot revive terminal offers; revised effects need a fresh offer and response. Failure changes no assets; success consumes once. Old-snapshot replay is a distinct branch, not a second settlement. J4's representation design is settled, with implementation unbuilt; J5 must assess natural-language stopping after every completed round, since deadlines, messages and offers can matter without changed material fields. These are requirements, not implemented capabilities.

Required design versus optional review. For this historical workload, omitting the optional reviewer gives about $0.05 rather than $0.54 per run. Require declaration checking, scripted tests and trace inspection. A valid but wrong rule shows their limits; it does not prove another model fixes the error. Existing reviewer findings remain advisory. A mandatory reviewer needs a named failure it corrects that the simpler path cannot handle. The judge’s confirmation step also remains unvalidated for semantic benefit.

Why the judge does not read the whole chain. One chain step averages 340 characters (2,081 steps measured in saved runs), so your 5,000-step example is about 500,000 tokens. The judge reads the net changes, the blockers and the chain's length and stopping point; the full chain stays in the run record and on the run page.
