People try anything; the world decides

Design for three missing pieces of World Substrate. Today a person in a World Builder world picks from a list of moves the rules already allow, so nobody ever walks into a wall. With this design they say what they try in their own words, the world's rules decide what happens, knock-on effects run to the end on their own, and a person's recorded skill decides how a risky attempt turns out. Pick an example to see what the run's record would show.

Blue, solid: in World Substrate now (S1 added attempts and interpreting) Orange, dashed: added by a later slice (S2 knock-ons, S3 rolls) Grey: what the person meant (kept, never read by rules)
1. Decidehave
The person's AI model writes what they believe, what they expect and why. These stay in their memory and the record.

2. Say the attemptbuilt in S1
One sentence of what they physically do. The list of known moves is shown as a hint, not a limit.
↓
3. Interpretbuilt in S1
A small AI call names which kind of move it is and who or what each part refers to, quoting the words it used. It cannot say whether the move works.
↓
4. Checkhave
The world's rules check the move. A failed check refuses it, names the cause, and changes nothing.
↓
5. Skill rollnew, piece 3
Only for risky moves the rules mark. Dice count comes from the person's recorded skill; the dice are recorded.
↓
6. Carry outhave
The rule's effects change the world, all at once or not at all.
↓
7. Knock-on effects to the endnew, piece 2
Rules marked "immediately" fire on the new state, again and again, until nothing more happens, all in the same moment. Each cites what caused it.
↓
8. Who noticeshave
Only people who could see or hear it learn of it.

What could go wrong, and what catches it

RiskCatch
The interpreter turns "fly the helicopter" into a safe walkIt must quote the words behind each part; a test checks this exact case; the run's read-through samples ordinary attempts
The interpreter decides what succeedsIts answer has no place to say success or failure; only the rules refuse
Fluent wording makes an untrained pilot land safelyThe roll reads only the recorded skill; the attempt's words never reach it
A knock-on effect feeds itself foreverEach rule fires once per thing per moment, at most 32 in all; over the cap the whole moment is refused and nothing changes
Replays of saved worlds changeInterpretations and dice are stored, so a replay never calls the AI or rolls again; all existing replay tests must pass

The types the world is built from

Every thing, move, rule and notice the steps above use is one of these types. Blue "built" means World Substrate has it today; orange "planned" means a later slice of this plan adds it. The last column says how each one maps onto the shared scientific-hypergraph kernel (revision 7b0638f), so the types are not a framework made up here. Before minting them, these formalisms were checked: PDDL (closest: a move kind is a PDDL action), Event Calculus (source of the knock-on design), onto-canon6, and the continuous-equation formats; Petri nets and TypeDB are not checked yet (open question Q24). Action kind (effector) builtSomething an actor can try, with named participant roles, checks and effects, compiled before use.specializes sci:RelationType

Effect builtA change to one component path that runs when every check holds, inside the declared write scope.no_counterpart
Declared write scope builtThe paths a rule may change; the Engine refuses any change outside them.no_counterpart
Process (knock-on rule) builtA rule the world runs by itself each round for every matching entity whose checks hold; this is how effects propagate (plants dry out, lessons arrive).no_counterpart

One choice for you

Decision: when someone tries something no rule covers, should an AI game master be allowed to invent a rough effect, marked "coarse" in the record? Recommendation: not yet. For now the person is told "nothing comes of it" and the attempt is listed so the next build's rules writer can add a real rule. That keeps every effect coming from a written rule. If you don't answer, it stays that way and slices 1 to 3 go ahead.
