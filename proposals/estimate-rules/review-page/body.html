Estimate rules: some things in a world happen by chance, and Jev estimates the chance

Status (2026-11-10): complete draft. You approved planning it on 2026-11-10 ("i approve"), in parallel with the AI judge build. Nothing is built yet, and nothing on this page waits on you.

Why. You said, 2026-11-10: "im not saying jsut the judge uses jev. i can see there being a way where we have certain types of rules/contstatins that progagte base don jev". Today every rule in a world is certain: if its checks pass, its effects happen. An estimate rule lets an outcome depend on a chance that depends on the facts (a blade not serviced for two weeks is more likely to break today), asks Jev for that chance, rolls against it, and records both. Jev supplies an estimate; only the declared authority can change state. This mechanics-estimate path does not choose for a person. A separately declared actor policy may use Jev when adequate for that decision.

This page is the plan's own package. The AI judge plan is on AI Judge Plan; the repository-wide package is on World Substrate: planning package. All share one set of checks, questions and models.

1. The examples: follow one day at the sawmill

Blue, solid: in the World Builder now Orange, dashed: added by this plan (planned, not built) Grey: what happens today

The sawmill. "A small sawmill: the saw cuts logs into boards while its blade is sound. The blade wears; the longer since it was serviced and the harder it is worked, the more likely it breaks. Mara, the sawyer, can service it, which takes the day. When the blade breaks, the saw stops, boards stop coming, and the buyer's order runs late. Logs come by cart across a ford, which is too deep to cross after heavy rain." Pick a case; the steps it passes through light up on the left. The numbers are an illustration of what the run page will show, not a run.

1. The rule's ordinary checkshave
A process the world runs each day (or a move a person tries) checks its conditions as today: the blade is sound and the saw is running. If a check fails, nothing is asked and nothing changes.
↓
2. Asked before in this run?new, E1
The same question with the same facts gets the answer already recorded in this run, so Jev is asked once per distinct question.
↓
3. Ask Jev a fixed question about named factsnew, E1
"Given the blade's days since service and yesterday's load, what is the chance it fails today?" Jev sees only those two facts, never what anyone said or thought. It answers a chance between 0 and 1. If it fails twice, the rule's own fallback applies (a chance the rule declared, or refusing the day), shown in orange.
↓
4. Roll against the chancenew, E1 (same seed as the skill roll)
The engine draws a number between 0 and 1 from the run's seed and this event. Under the chance: it happens. Never "it happens because the chance is high".
↓
5. Record both on the eventnew, E1
The question, the facts with their values, Jev's answer, the model version, the call's trace id and cost, whether it was reused, and the roll.
↓
6. The effects and every knock-on followhave
The rule's "if it happens" effects apply, and the same-moment knock-on effects run as for any rule, each step citing the step that caused it.
↓
7. People decide what to do about ithave
Mara's own resident decides whether to service the blade. No chance is ever estimated for what a person chooses.

4. Decided (the agent's calls, as working defaults)

Your words set the direction; these design points were worked out from reading the code and prior art (inferred by agent, 2026-11-10). None needs your answer; each can be vetoed.

#DecisionBasis
1An estimate is an input, never a fact. The rule declares what the estimate means and how it is used. A fresh event chance may be sampled; an unknown persistent condition must retain its identity and lifetime rather than be resampled on every query. Classifications and policy judgments have separate declared decision rules. An estimator never writes canonical state by itself.Open question Q63's contract; the retired Jev note, "borrow estimators; own reality"
2Three kinds, matching Jev's three answer types: a chance roll (yes or no), a pick among the rule's own options, and an amount from the rule's own list of values. For declared stochastic events, sample their distribution; do not replace a chance with a threshold. This is not a universal sampling rule for classifications, persistent unknowns or actor-policy decisions.llm_client's typed decisions (NoulQuestion, ChoiceQuestion, ScoreQuestion); "Do LLMs Play Dice?" (COLING 2025): models state chances better than they sample them
2bRules and constraints. On a process (a rule the world runs) the outcome's effects follow. On a move (a constraint on what a person can do) a losing roll may block the move: it is refused whole, with the chance and roll named as the blocker.Your "rules/contstatins"; the judge plan's split, where a constraint is what the world makes possible or impossible
3What a rule declares: a fixed question, the facts it reads (nothing else reaches Jev), the answer type, the model, a required fallback (refuse, or a declared value) and the effects for each result. No chance roll without stakes.The skill roll's rules (docs/plans/open_attempt.md:188-194); architecture section 8: a missing authority refuses visibly unless a fallback is declared
4One seed shared with the skill roll. The roll comes from the run's seed and the event; whichever of the two features lands first builds the seed.docs/plans/open_attempt.md:192; open question Q33
5Replay never calls Jev. It reads the recorded answers and stops if one is missing, or if the rule was edited so its question or facts no longer match the record.The existing recorded-reply replay (scripts/replay_build.py:23-49)
6Ask once per complete input per run. Reuse requires the same resolved model and estimator/prompt versions, question, answer schema/options and declared facts. Each distinct stochastic event draws its own number; repeated queries about one persistent condition reuse its recorded realization.The rule names every fact Jev reads, so the same facts mean the same question
7When Jev fails: one retry, then the rule's fallback, shown in orange. Each try is cut off after 5 seconds.Measured: 1,330 of 20,001 recorded Jev calls failed (6.6%); network failures waited 20 s on average, while 99% of successful answers came within 2.8 s
8The free test run never calls Jev. When the World Builder test-runs a draft's rules with simple moves, a stand-in answers each chance 1 and 0 in turn, so every outcome of every chance rule is tried, for free and the same every time, and the record says "stand-in estimate (test run)". A live run's Jev spend counts in the daily allowance like any other AI call (the illustrative blade component is about $0.00084 for 30 uncached questions before retries, excluding crossing estimates and other calls; this is not whole-run cost).The test run is a gate only because it is free and repeatable (scripts/world_builder_service.py:1845); the run's cost is already settled from its trace (:297, ledger :225-238); Q87

The harbourmaster. Whether he notices a hand-over across the quay is a chance about what the world shows him: an estimate can decide whether he sees it. What he does once he knows is his own choice, made by his resident, as whether Rosa takes the coins is hers (the judge plan's Decided 4). This is the last slice (E5), because it changes what a person learns rather than a fact about a thing.

The cheaper options, and how they are weighed. Two designs need no model call per run; compute and authoring costs remain. A formula written into the rule (chance = 3% per day since service) would need arithmetic the rule language does not have. Declared chances need nothing new: the rule's checks pick a chance the rules writer wrote, for example under 5 days since service 5%, 5 to 9 days 20%, 10 or more 40%, rolled the same way; this is the same path a rule's fallback already takes. Against them: every number is the rules writer's guess, fixed when the world is built, and the numbers multiply with the facts (3 facts in 3 steps each is 27 chances to write); and you asked for rules that propagate based on Jev. That is an argument, not proof, so E1 compares them: agreement within 0.1 on 8 of 10 cases is diagnostic only. Before preferring bands, E2 must justify applicability, dependencies, thresholds and intervention responses (Q88). Agreement does not establish interchangeability or correctness. The illustrative 30-query blade component costs under a tenth of a cent before crossing queries and workload variation. Speed matters more than cost: successful Jev answers averaged 0.43 s, but 6.6% of calls failed, network failures only after 20 s (measured). With a 5 s limit per try and one retry, a chance takes about 0.61 s on average and at most 10 s before its fallback, about 18 s for 30 blade queries (worked out from those shares; add crossing queries and orchestration overhead; Q85).

The plain version (planned for E1, not run). The workspace rule says an AI answer starts as the same model given the whole source. Here: Jev given the whole world rather than the two named facts. E1 compares, on 10 cases side by side, Jev with the named facts, Jev with the whole world, gpt-6-luna with the named facts, and the rules writer's declared chances (no Jev). The whole-world version is the record the rule asks for, not a design option: a rule never lets Jev read the whole world, because unrelated world changes would invalidate reuse and it could reach what a person said. Differences motivate inspecting dependencies and omitted mechanisms; they do not prove the whole-world answer is better. Add declared reads only when causal and intervention analysis justifies them (Q83). A 10-question probe (about $0.0003) checks whether answers respect the scenario's declared wear mechanism (Q82); rising chance is expected only if that mechanism justifies it; the existing probe's chances were coarse (0.6, 0.55). No Jev call was made for planning.

Could Jev serve the judge's plain-words stopping point? Possibly (the 1/50 cost ratio is a guess, not a measured stopping-workload comparison), but Jev would answer a chance, not stop or go, and has not been shown to read a whole world well. That is the judge plan's call for J5 (Q86); this plan only records the option.
