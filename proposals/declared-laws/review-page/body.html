Each world states its own laws; the engine holds the rules to them

Design for fixing the cause of the pencil-world bugs instead of one symptom at a time. Today the AI that writes a world's rules writes every move as raw field changes, and nothing compares those changes with what it knows, so a shop can "buy" boxes without paying (pencil13) and we add one more hand-written check per mistake. In this plan the AI that designs the world also writes the world's laws in its own terms ("pencils are made only by the factory"; "buying boxes moves boxes one way and cash the other"), the engine refuses any move that breaks one and names the law, and a separate AI reviews the rules against the description. Moves stay free: there is no fixed list of move types. Built so far (2026-10-30): S1 to S3, declared laws, the engine's law check and the review, on things worlds and, from this change, on people worlds too; S4 is in progress and S5 is not started. The examples below show the build before this plan and what it now records.

This page is the plan's own package. The repository-wide package is on World Substrate: planning package; both share one set of checks, questions and models (Q41).

2. Design the world and declare its lawsnew, S1
The AI that designs places, things and people also lists the world's laws in two shapes: a total only named moves may change, and a move that must move one thing each way. Each law has a plain sentence for the page.

3. Write the ruleshave; laws added as input
The rules writer writes each move's checks and effects, told every move must keep the laws.
↓
4. Independent reviewnew, S3
A different AI model, with a fresh context, reads the description, the laws and the rules, and lists concrete mismatches. It gives findings, never a pass mark.
↓
5. Test run, laws enforcedtest run have; law check new, S2
The build's free scripted run plays the rules. A step that breaks a law is refused whole, with the law named, and nothing changes.
↓
6. Fix what was foundhave; inputs new
The rules writer rewrites only the moves named by a law refusal or a review finding (the "changed moves only" retry from pull request 278).
↓
7. Keep the world and run ithave; law check stays on
People act each round. The engine keeps checking the laws on every move and knock-on effect.

Which checks stay and which go

The build has about fourteen hand-written checks, each added after one bad world. Checks that report what the free test run actually saw are observations, so they stay. Checks that guess a rule's meaning from its shape are what laws and the review replace; each goes only when the saved worlds of the incidents it caught are covered (open question Q45).

What could go wrong, and what catches it

RiskCatch
Laws turn into fixed move types by the back doorLaws only constrain totals and pairings of the world's own fields; moves are written freely (requirement R1)
The designer declares a wrong law, so an honest move is refusedThe refusal shows in the build record with the law's sentence, and both the rules writer and the review see it; S5's reads look for this
The designer forgets a lawA missing law catches less, never more wrongly; the review still reads the rules against the description
The review just agrees with the rules writerA different model family, a fresh context, findings not a verdict, and its value measured on the saved incident worlds before any check retires (Q44)
A rule that is not a simple total (a price formula, stock with nowhere to go)Not a law: an ordinary rule the review reads, and the test run reports stock that only grew (Q43, settled by reading every logged incident and its saved world)
The same thing sits under different names (the customer's cash, the shop's business.cash)A law names a resource declared once with every field it is kept in, never a raw field (Q47)
Saved worlds changeA world with no laws runs exactly as today; every replay test must pass on each merge
