One loop for every kind of thing in the world

Anyone or anything may try anything. The world's rules, not a menu, decide what happens, and knock-on effects keep firing on their own. Each step below is labelled with the established framework it borrows from. Pick an example to trace it through the loop.

Blue, solid: World Substrate already has this Orange, dashed: missing; copy the named framework
1. Decidehave: CognitionAdapter
A person, in-world AI, animal, machine or firm chooses what to try. Its decider may be an AI model, a fast simple model, or plain code, and it says how faithful it is.
From: Russell & Norvig agent types; Levin's axis of persuadability; Concordia's swappable acting component
What it meant to happen is kept in its memory and in the trace for us. No world rule ever reads it.
↓
2. Attemptmissing: today residents pick from a list
It uses one of its effectors: voice, legs, hands, or thought aimed at itself. No effector, nothing happens. Nothing is refused at this point.
From: Inform 7 "try"; Voyager (attempts go to an engine that decides)
↓
3. Check the world's ruleshave: mechanic checks, held-fact gates
Each thing's rules are checked: a door opens only if unlocked; you can dial only a number you hold. A failed check is recorded as a refusal with its reason.
From: Inform 7 check rules; DEVS external transition; BFO disposition
↓
4. Skill roll, only when there is something at stakemissing
The game master judges how risky and how big. Code rolls against the recorded skill level, not against how expert the words sound. Untrained means a worse roll, not a ban.
From: Blades in the Dark action roll; GURPS skill defaults; BFO capability
↓
5. Carry outhave: mechanics propose changes
The matched rule proposes changes to the world. It does not write them yet.
From: Inform 7 carry-out rules; PDDL action effects
↓
6. Knock-on effects, repeated until quietmissing: only within one rule today
Standing rules react to the proposed change: unsupported things fall, falling glass breaks, heard words reach listeners. Repeat until nothing new fires.
From: C+ / BC+ static laws; Event Calculus triggers; PDDL+ events; Flecs observers
↓
7. One commit, or one refusalhave: the governed-rules layer
All the changes land together or none do, each with its recorded cause and its declared write scope checked.
From: World Substrate today (Decision 006); same shape as Orchestrated Reality and IVIE (2026)
↓
8. Who noticeshave: information delivery
Effects travel along channels with limited reach: sound to nearby ears, sight within view. Each decider sees only what reached it, then decides again.
From: Russell & Norvig sensors; DEVS ports and coupling
Things that act on their own, like a clock, wake on a timer and enter at step 2 (DEVS internal transition). Things that never act, like a chair or a wall, are records that other things' rules check.
An attempt that no rule matches: either an existing rule fails it (the wall), or the game master decides, marked "coarse" and flagged so a rule can be written later. Only tabletop games handle this; nothing in software does.
Source: Flexible simulator prior art, 2026-10 (survey of formal theory, games and interactive fiction, and AI-agent platforms, with sources and unverified claims marked). This is a proposal, not a decision.
