Prototype Pump Validation

Ari is the validation engineer, Mara the coordinator, and Bo a member operating a prototype pump. There is one concern: whether it is safe to activate. Keep actual technical condition, reports/provenance, each recipient's exposure, personal interpretation/support, recorded assent, and institutional activation hold distinct. Initially the pump genuinely fails validation. Only Ari knows the private detail: sensor channel C drifted 8 units in validation batch V17. Mara knows the public procedure and receives a short report saying validation failed, without the private channel/batch detail. Bo knows the procedure but has received no failure report. Give each person their own profile and memory. They can independently request evidence, investigate, ignore a claim, retain support, dissent or record assent. Attributable reports are delivered to named recipients; undelivered reports reveal nothing. Ari may choose whether and whom to tell. Under the public procedure, filing a validation hold publicly reveals failed validation but never private test details. A false warning cannot change the pump's actual condition. Completing a review may confirm failure; neither review completion nor waiting repairs anything. Ari can perform an explicit technical repair and then authorized revalidation. Passing revalidation clears only the technical hold; activation still requires Mara's own recorded assent and Bo's own recorded assent. No message or rule forces persuasion, support change, assent or a particular ending. Limit the scenario to these three people, one pump, one concern, reports, repair/revalidation and activation.
