An AI judge that rules only on what no rule covers, fed by everything the engine works out

Status (2026-11-09): complete draft. You approved its direction on 2026-11-09 ("i approve", after Decided 5). Nothing is built yet, and nothing on this page waits on you.

Why. You said, 2026-11-09: "an ai adjudicator doesnt really scale and that we need to facts to have implications and blockers. like 50 things could propagate down a causal chain 100 links deep taht the llm would not be able to understand so we need that causal chain to automaticlaly rpogate and to deliver the resutls of that propagation to he ai judge along with blockers." So the engine does the bookkeeping, however long the chain; the judge only fills gaps, and each ruling becomes a rule everyone is then held to. Other people's choices stay theirs.

1. The examples: follow one attempt

Blue, solid: in the World Builder now Orange, dashed: added by this plan (planned, not built) Grey: what happens today

The port. "A small port: Rosa, the customs officer, signs inspection releases. Vik, a trader, has 40 coins and a crate he has not declared. The exit gate is a barcode scanner that opens for any valid release code. Beyond the gate, a 100-step chain runs: the crate reaches the warehouse, the warehouse fills, the dispatcher sends a truck..." Pick a case; the steps it passes through light up on the left.

1. A person tries somethinghave
A Concordia resident says what it tries in its own words; the interpreter reads it against the installed moves.
↓
2. Does a rule cover it?have
If an installed move fits, the rulebook decides, as today; if one of its checks fails, the attempt is refused with the blocker named and the judge is not asked. Today an attempt nothing fits changes nothing.
↓
3. Pass 1: what the engine knows without a rulenew, J1
Nothing has happened yet, so there is nothing to propagate. The engine lists the moves open to the person acting and to each person the attempt names, with which checks pass or fail (engine.discover for each), and the current values of every thing the attempt names. In the port, Rosa's list holds "sign release: blocked, inspected is false"; Vik's list could not show it.
↓
4. Someone else's choice goes to themnew, J2 (today only plain requests, #326)
If the attempt needs another person to act (accept, sign, refuse), that person's own resident decides. No judge, no rule.
↓
5. The judge proposes a rulenew, J2
Concordia's own game master, given the whole world, the attempt and pass 1, proposes a ruling on what the world makes possible (can coins change hands here?), written as a rule that applies to everyone in that situation. It never writes an outcome.
↓
6. Pass 2: the engine test-runs that rulenew, J0 and J1
The rules check, then a test run on a copy of the world: every knock-on effect, however long the chain, and every blocker. The judge reads the result and confirms, revises once, or withdraws. If no rule the language allows can express the ruling, the attempt is refused: "no rule could express this".
↓
7. The rule is installed, or heldnew, J3
Installed at once and the attempt settles through it (default), or held with the attempt pending until you press Approve (installs, settles) or Reject (refuses), per the world's setting. Nothing settles without an installed rule.
↓
8. From then on the rulebook decides ithave, with the new rule; J0 lifts the chain cap
The same attempt by anyone is decided by the rule, with no judge call; every knock-on runs to the end in the same moment, each step citing its cause.

The two-pass judge (inferred by agent, Q78). An attempt no rule covers has not happened, so there is nothing yet to propagate. So the judge first gets what the engine knows without a rule (pass 1), proposes a rule, and then sees the engine's full test run of that rule (pass 2) before anything is installed. That is how your "deliver the resutls of that propagation to he ai judge" is met.

A gate a guard opens is not a mechanism: it opens when the guard decides to. Cybernetic Influence v3 already kept identity proof, permission, availability and outcome apart, but did not record who holds a credential and left social engineering out of scope; this plan adds both.

The cheaper option, and why it is not taken. Your Decided 3 asks that a ruling pass the existing rules check. The model review is not part of passing: it only returns findings. Dropping it for judge rulings would cost about $0.05 per run instead of $0.54. It is kept because the free checks let a valid but wrong rule through (the sawmill case below) and the review is the one step that reads a rule against the world's description. J2 tries it on the sawmill rule and drops it if it misses the price.

Why the judge does not read the whole chain. One chain step averages 340 characters (2,081 steps measured in saved runs), so your 5,000-step example is about 500,000 tokens. The judge reads the net changes, the blockers and the chain's length and stopping point; the full chain stays in the run record and on the run page.
