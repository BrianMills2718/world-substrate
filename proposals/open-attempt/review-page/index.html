People try anything; the world decides

Design for three missing pieces of World Substrate. The first is built: since slice 1 (merged 2026-10-25) a person in a World Builder world says what they try in their own words instead of picking from a list, and only the world's rules decide what happens, so walking into a wall is refused with the wall named. Still to come: knock-on effects that run to the end on their own (slice 2), and a person's recorded skill deciding how a risky attempt turns out (slice 3). The first live airfield run with AI residents (2026-10-26) showed residents moving every round and the wall refused by name, but no resident reached the helicopter, so the skill and knock-on cases were not yet exercised live; slice 1 is built, not done. Pick an example to see what the run's record shows, or will show.

The world's rules check the move. A failed check refuses it, names the cause, and changes nothing.
↓
5. Skill rollnew, piece 3
Only for risky moves the rules mark. Dice count comes from the person's recorded skill; the dice are recorded.
↓
6. Carry outhave
The rule's effects change the world, all at once or not at all.
↓
7. Knock-on effects to the endnew, piece 2
Rules marked "immediately" fire on the new state, again and again, until nothing more happens, all in the same moment. Each cites what caused it.
↓

Only people who could see or hear it learn of it.

RiskCatch

The types the world is built from

Every thing, move, rule and notice the steps above use is one of these types. Blue "built" means World Substrate has it today; orange "planned" means a later slice of this plan adds it. The last column says how each one maps onto the shared scientific-hypergraph kernel (revision 7b0638f), so the types are not a framework made up here. Before minting them, these formalisms were checked: PDDL and PDDL+ (closest: a move kind is a PDDL action, and PDDL+ has processes and events), Event Calculus (source of the knock-on design), onto-canon6, and the repository's own projection onto Linguistic Core's relation-schema.v1. The continuous-equation formats (SBML, CellML, Modelica and others) were not checked further: they hold continuous equations, and these rules are discrete checks and effects. Petri nets and TypeDB were weighed and composed rather than adopted (Q24, resolved): Petri-net transition semantics are reused for linked process participants, but neither carries per-observer noticing or named refusals.
